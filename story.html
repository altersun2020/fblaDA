FBLA Data Analysis · 2026–2027 · Flinders Street Technology

Americans are coming back to civic life. Where should FST invest?

Four surveys, 17 behaviors, 50 states. One story in eight charts.

AmeriCorps & U.S. Census Bureau, Civic Engagement and Volunteering (CEV) Supplement 2017–2023 · Independent Sector · CECP · BLS. Every chart was built by the competitors from the source tables.

1 · The big picture

Almost everyone talks. Half help a neighbor. Only 1 in 4 volunteers through an organization.

Share of Americans 16+ who did each at least once, Sept 2022–Sept 2023. Color = CEV engagement area. Source: AmeriCorps CEV national rates. Visual 1.
97% talk with friends and family; 70% with neighbors
54% help neighbors informally, 49% give to charity
28% formally volunteer, the behavior FST can move most
2 · What changed

The pandemic knocked down in-person engagement. Most of it is back; interest in issues is not.

Percentage-point change in each national measure. Employer encouragement starts in 2021. Source: AmeriCorps CEV. Visual 2.
Rebounded: volunteering +5.1, taking action with neighbors +3.5, informal helping +3.4
Still falling: following the news −5.7 since 2017, posting views −4.4, contacting officials −2.7
Steady climbers: boycotts/buycotts +3.6, employer encouragement +4.5 in two years
3 · Twenty years of volunteering

Flat for two decades, a record low in 2021, then the fastest rebound ever measured.

Share of Americans who volunteered through an organization. Grey: BLS/Census CPS Volunteer Supplement 2003–2015. Teal: CEV 2017–2023. Dashed gap = 2017 questionnaire redesign; the two series are not directly comparable. Visual 3.
2021: 23.2%, lowest ever recorded
2023: 28.3%, +5.1 pts, a 22% jump in two years
Gap left: 1.7 pts below 2019
4 · Who engages

Volunteering rises with income and education. The biggest untapped groups are young, lower-income and Hispanic Americans.

Source: AmeriCorps CEV rates by demographic subgroup. Try: Generation, Ethnicity, Education. Visual 4 (interactive).
2.5× gap: 16% volunteer in households under $25k vs 41% at $150k+
Drop-off at 18: 34% of 16–17 year-olds volunteer, 23% of 18–24
Millennials are above their pre-pandemic rate; Gen Z giving is up 18% → 24%

    8 · The plan

    Four priorities, each tied to a number FST can track.

    1

    Ask every employee High feasibility

    Paid volunteer time off, 1:1 gift matching, a company-wide service week.

    Why: encouraged workers volunteer at 42% vs 23%Benchmark: 93% of large firms match gifts; avg participation 25%KPI: participation ≥ 31% (small-company benchmark)
    2

    Build the hybrid-volunteering layer High feasibility

    Free scheduling, remote-mentoring and IT tools for nonprofits; engineers on virtual pro bono projects.

    Why: online volunteers serve 95 hrs vs 64Benchmark: 82% of large firms offer virtual volunteeringKPI: hybrid hours delivered; nonprofits onboarded
    3

    Reach the 18–24s and lower-income workers Medium feasibility

    Paid tech-skills service fellowships with community colleges and Hispanic-serving institutions.

    Why: volunteering drops 34% → 23% after age 17Why: <$25k households 16%; Hispanic 17%KPI: fellows placed; volunteering 12 months later
    4

    Fund neighbors in the lagging states Medium feasibility

    Community grants for mutual-aid networks and tool libraries in the 11 states still 5+ points below 2019 and the Sun Belt metros.

    Why: informal helping at a record 54%Target: SD, WV, CO, NM, RI, OH, MO, WI, AL, NC, MS; MiamiKPI: state rates vs 2019 at the next CEV wave
    Budget rule
    1% of pre-tax profit
    CECP field standard; median large company gives 0.78%
    Year 1
    VTO + matching + 3 pilot nonprofits
    baseline employee survey using CEV wording
    Year 2
    Platform beta + first fellows + grants open
    two lagging states first
    Every 2 years
    Re-benchmark on the new CEV wave
    move money to where the gap vs 2019 is largest

    Method in five lines

    • Percentage points vs. percent: 23.2% → 28.3% is +5.1 points and +22%.
    • Baseline: every state is compared with its own 2019 pre-pandemic rate.
    • Correlation: Pearson r across 51 states; describes association, not cause.
    • Value: hours × Independent Sector value of a volunteer hour.
    • Caveat: the 2017 redesign moved the volunteering question; pre- and post-2017 series are shown but never compared directly. Survey estimates carry sampling error, wider at state level.
