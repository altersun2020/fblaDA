Practice Q&A

Questions judges are likely to ask, with data-backed answers

Why is 2021 so low? Is that just COVID?

Mostly. The CEV asked about Sept 2020–Sept 2021, when organizations suspended in-person programs. Formal volunteering fell 6.8 points to 23.2%, the lowest ever recorded, but people who kept volunteering served at the same intensity, and informal helping barely moved (50.9%). The 2023 rebound of 5.1 points is the fastest growth in the series.

Why can't you compare 2015 (24.9%) with 2017 (30.3%) directly?

In 2017 the Census Bureau moved the volunteering question from the start to the end of the supplement. AmeriCorps says the jump is at least partly an "order effect," so we treat 2003–2015 and 2017–2023 as two series and only compare within each.

Correlation of 0.63 between employer encouragement and volunteering: does that prove the program works?

No. It is a cross-sectional correlation across 51 states and could reflect state culture or industry mix. We pair it with AmeriCorps' individual-level finding that 42% of workers at encouraging employers volunteer vs. 23% nationally in 2021, and we recommend a baseline survey and KPIs so FST can measure its own effect.

Charitable giving is down. Should FST focus on donations instead of volunteering?

Giving fell from 52.2% to 48.5% and is down in 38 of 51 states since 2017, but it is still the most common form of organizational engagement and ticked up for the first time in 2023. Our Priority 1 includes a matching-gift program, and the data shows giving and volunteering move together (r = 0.80 across states), so growing one tends to grow the other.

Why target informal helping if a company can't measure it?

Because it is where Americans are actually growing: 54.3% in 2023, a record, and 12.9 million more helpers than 2021. The CEV does measure it every two years at state level, so FST can benchmark grant regions against the national trend.

How much would this cost?

CECP's field standard is 1% of pre-tax profit; the median large company gave 0.78% in 2024 and the median total was $21.5 million. FST is fictional, so we size budgets as formulas: volunteer time off and matching as a fixed share of U.S. payroll; the hybrid platform mostly in-kind engineering time; fellowships and grants as a capped annual pool. Priorities 1 and 2 use assets the company already owns, which is why they are rated high feasibility.

What is the economic value based on?

Hours served multiplied by Independent Sector's value of a volunteer hour, $33.49 in 2023. Hours actually fell from about 5.8 billion in 2019 to 4.99 billion in 2023; the dollar value rose from $147B to $167.2B because the hourly value increased.

What about the other 31 countries?

The CEV is U.S.-only, so our plan is U.S.-scoped as the brief asked. We recommend replicating the benchmark approach using each country's national statistics (UK Community Life Survey, Statistics Canada GSS, Australia GSS). Globally, UNV estimates about 862 million people volunteer monthly, roughly 70% informally, which mirrors the U.S. pattern that informal helping outnumbers formal volunteering.

Which groups should FST worry about most?

Lower-income households (16.3% volunteer under $25k vs. 41.4% at $150k+), Hispanic Americans (16.9%), and 18–24 year-olds (22.6%, down from 34.1% at ages 16–17). Those are also the groups least likely to have an employer that encourages service, so a corporate program alone will not reach them; hence Priority 3.

Where did the data come from and how reliable is it?

The Current Population Survey CEV Supplement, run by the U.S. Census Bureau for AmeriCorps every two years, with tens of thousands of households per wave and weighted, nationally representative estimates. State estimates have wider margins of error, which is why we look for patterns across many states rather than single-state changes.
